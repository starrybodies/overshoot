# OVERSHOOT launch film: master generation prompt

Use this as the **style bible** for every generated shot. Do not submit it as a single 70-second generation. Generate the shots in [`SHOT-LIST.md`](SHOT-LIST.md) one at a time, and paste this document ahead of each shot prompt. Attach that shot's first-frame and last-frame images.

Record the voiceover separately from [`VO-SCRIPT.md`](VO-SCRIPT.md). The video model must not speak, and it must not render the VO lines or the titles as on-screen text. Titles are set in the edit.

---

## The film

A breathtaking 60–75 second cinematic launch film for **OVERSHOOT**, a Gaia AI product.

OVERSHOOT is a living planetary atlas of humanity's material metabolism: extraction → refinement → processing → transport → manufacturing → sale → use → discard → recovery.

Its central question is also the headline on its homepage:

> **WHERE DID IT COME FROM?**
> **WHERE DID IT GO?**

This must NOT feel like a startup explainer, a generic climate-tech advertisement, a corporate sustainability film or a conventional product demo. It should feel as if we have suddenly gained the ability to see the hidden metabolism of civilization.

The film moves seamlessly between three scales:

- **THE EARTH:** planetary systems, mines, forests, cities, ports, oceans, infrastructure
- **THE MATERIAL:** ore, molecules, metals, petroleum, plastics, components, products, waste
- **THE MAP:** the real OVERSHOOT interface, which shows how these physical systems connect

The signature visual language is a seamless transition between DATA and REALITY. A point on the map expands into a physical place. Particles become crushed ore. A line becomes a sea lane. Then everything pulls back out until the journey becomes one luminous thread across the living Earth.

Think planetary documentary × scientific visualization × refined contemporary title design × satellite intelligence × microscopic material photography × premium speculative cinema.

## Everything should feel physical

Stone has weight. Ore has texture. Ships have scale. Factories have heat. Cities consume staggering quantities of matter. Waste has consequence.

## Avoid

Cheesy sci-fi holograms, fake HUD interfaces, generic green-tech imagery, excessive neon, stock-footage montage aesthetics, corporate handshakes, floating sustainability icons, cyberpunk clichés, fake data, invented UI, and legible invented text, numbers, logos or place names anywhere in frame.

## Visual direction

- Photoreal physical environments. True geological texture. Macro material photography. Realistic industrial scale. Satellite imagery. Elegant cartography.
- Deep blacks and mineral whites. Earth-derived colour, punctuated by the real OVERSHOOT palette. Copper is `#b85226` in the atlas, so let copper light carry that hue.
- Material-flow graphics should feel almost physical: particles, trajectories, density, accumulation and transformation, never arbitrary glowing lines.
- Camera movement creates impossible continuous relationships between scales: microscopic mineral → mine → landscape → planet → map → route → ship → port → factory → object → waste → material → planet.
- Match cut on geometry and material wherever possible. Ore particle → map node. Circular mine → circular port. Molten copper → illuminated route. Container grid → city grid. Circuit traces → trade routes. Landfill contours → topographic map.
- The edit should progressively reveal that these apparently separate scenes are ONE SYSTEM.

## Product integration (non-negotiable)

- The supplied OVERSHOOT screen captures in `plates/` are canonical. Preserve their exact typography, logo, controls, map styling, colours, data and layout.
- Never generate, redesign, reinterpret or "enhance" an interface. Generated shots may *begin* or *end* on a supplied still (first/last frame), but the interface itself is always the real recording, composited in the edit.
- At key moments the interface occupies the entire frame, so viewers understand that this is a **real product they can use**.

## Evidence discipline

OVERSHOOT's credibility is that it separates what is measured from what is assumed. The film may use metaphor, but it must not make claims the atlas itself refuses to make:

- **The one continuous copper journey is a metaphor, not a tracked shipment.** The atlas does not trace one wire back to one mine, and it says so. No on-screen text, VO or label should imply that it does.
- **Map lines are not vehicle tracks.** The home globe's curves join country centres. When a line becomes a ship, it is a transition between two ways of seeing, not a claim that the line is that ship's route. No generated ship, port or mine carries a real name, logo or livery.
- **Keep the materials physically right.** Copper concentrate travels as dark bulk sand: by slurry pipeline to the coast and by bulk carrier at sea. Cathodes are stacked sheets. Containers belong to the manufactured-goods leg, such as electronics. Use the container-stack match cut there, not on the concentrate.
- **Not everything is recycled.** Waste pathways diverge: recovered, stockpiled, burned, leaked, exported, buried.
- Real places in the plates are fine because they are sourced records. Escondida's sourced connections to the Chilean coast (plate P04) and the Highland Valley Copper → Horne → CCR copper journey (P05) are real atlas views. Generated footage around them should look *like* such places, never *be* them.

## Sound

Begin almost microscopically: stone movement, metal resonance, subterranean rumble. Build rhythm from the material system itself: crusher impacts, rail joints, furnace roar, container locks, ship horns, factory machinery, scanner beeps, sorting machinery. Turn those industrial sounds into percussion and build to an enormous but sophisticated electronic crescendo as the planetary network is revealed. Then strip almost everything away for "Because there is no away," leaving one low planetary resonance. End with a sonic signature on the OVERSHOOT mark. No generic inspirational corporate music.

## Tone

Awe without apocalypse. Urgency without preaching. Intelligence without academic dryness. Technology without techno-utopianism. Beauty without disguising consequence. The Earth is neither victim nor abstraction. Civilization is revealed as what it physically is: a planetary transformation of matter. OVERSHOOT gives us a way to see it.

The viewer should leave thinking *"I have never thought about the physical world this way,"* and then, immediately, *"I need to open this map."*
